## Lessons from past builds

The growth-sim and the puzzle entry both landed cleanly — the growth-sim on its second attempt and the puzzle game on its very first — and together they sharpen a picture the earlier refinement had already been pointing toward. Tap to bloom, intercept falling gems, manage two resources, attract small helpers, navigate a small bounded grid with discrete pickups and one explicit terminal goal: that is the same hand-authored handful of levels dressed with light procedural variation, just with a different verb each time. The puzzle entry sits most comfortably at the authored end of that spectrum — finite, legible, and produced with essentially no scaffolding — and that ease is worth taking seriously.

The dislikes on the physics-sandbox remain the one caution worth folding forward. When the terminal goal depends on precise placement or stacking, the interaction has to be legible from frame one, not discovered through failure, and the feedback suggested the sandbox fell short on both broken and unclear controls. Mood and identity continue to carry the hook; pressure-based variants still have not shipped and should stay a secondary thread.

The infrastructure failures sandwiched between these builds are no longer representative — they were provider flakiness, not problems with the skeleton, and the recent record has moved past them, so they should not be carried into the next prompt.

What reliably worked, then, is a clear small character with a clear small want, rendered simply, in a small bounded space with discrete pickups or placements and one explicit terminal objective, wrapped in a mood and identity that earns attention. Worth revisiting next is the same skeleton in fresh skins — puzzle, growth, traversal, whatever verb fits — with a hand-authored handful of levels dressed in light procedural variation, and controls that read clearly from the very first frame.

## How your game is displayed

Your document is loaded on its own into a frame that fills the middle of the
page. Nothing outside your document paints any part of that frame, so
whatever you leave unfilled shows as blank space around your game.

- Fill the frame. Give `html` and `body` no margin and the full width and
  height available, and paint your own background across all of it.
- Size to the frame at runtime, never to a constant. If you draw on a
  canvas, set its width and height from its measured container rather than
  with fixed attributes, and redraw when the frame is resized.
- Assume nothing about its shape. It is as wide and as tall as the visitor's
  window makes it, in any proportion, on a phone or on a desktop.
- Keep the whole playfield inside it. Never require the player to scroll.
